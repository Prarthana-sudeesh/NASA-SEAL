## 1. The game in one paragraph

The player has just been **promoted to aerospace engineer at NASA**. Mission Control gives them a job: pick an exoplanet from NASA's catalogue, design a rocket that can fly there **and come home** (Earth → planet → Earth), stay within budget, and launch. Hand-drawn cutscenes show what happens. Afterwards the game explains, in plain words and real numbers, exactly why the mission worked or failed. Each run is **10 launches** on one difficulty; the **average score of the 10** goes on a public leaderboard showing **name, average score and difficulty**.

## 5. Screens and flow

2. **Promotion briefing**: Cass congratulates the player on their promotion to aerospace engineer and explains the job in 4–5 short, skippable bubbles. Asks for an engineer name (used later for the leaderboard; editable).
3. **Choose difficulty**: three big cards: **Cadet** (Easy), **Engineer** (Medium), **Flight Director** (Hard), each with budget, what's new at that level, and a sample planet.
4. **Choose destination**: a "star map" carousel/grid of planet cards (section 7). Filters: distance, temperature, size. Each card: procedural planet, **nickname** in large playful type, **official NASA designation** directly underneath, distance in light-years, temperature, gravity, a difficulty-appropriate "challenge" tag.
5. **Mission brief**: full planet sheet with two clearly separated panels: **"Real NASA data"** (with source line) and **"Game assumptions"**. Then the computed mission requirements shown simply (e.g. "You'll need about 14 km/s of rocket power for the chemical parts of the trip").
6. **Rocket workshop** (the core screen; section 8).
7. **Pre-launch check**: checklist with ticks/crosses, pressure gauge, budget bar, crew count and the astronaut multiplier risk shown clearly. "GO / NO-GO" poll animation from Mission Control.
8. **Launch**: 10-second countdown with `countdown_beep.wav` each second and `countdown_go.wav` at zero (the big button press is the audio-unlock gesture), then the cutscene sequence (section 6). A mission-status tracker overlays the top: Launch → Transit → Arrival → Surface/Orbit → Return → Home.
9. **Mission report**: outcome stamp (MISSION SUCCESS / MISSION LOST), score breakdown counting up with `score_tick.wav`, and **"Why it happened"** (section 10.4).
10. **Run progress**: attempt N/10, running average, best attempt, "Next launch". After launch 10: run summary + leaderboard submission.
11. **Leaderboard** (section 9). **Settings**: master/music/SFX volume, reduced motion ("Calm mode"), haptics on/off, text size, reset progress.

Persist the in-progress run (difficulty, attempts, scores, current design) in `localStorage` so a refresh doesn't lose it.

## 6. Cutscenes, sound and haptics

### 6.1 Playback engine
Build a `CutscenePlayer` that plays a **sequence** of clips seamlessly:
- Two stacked `<video playsinline preload="auto">` elements (double-buffer): while one plays, the next is loaded and paused on its first frame; swap on `ended` so there is never a black flash.
- Preload all four clips when the player enters the workshop.
- Read `cutscenes.json` for durations and cue points. Support `mirror: true` per segment (CSS `transform: scaleX(-1)`) and `stopAt: seconds` (pause at a time, then hold the frame).
- If `play()` with sound is rejected, retry muted and show a small "Tap for sound" chip.
- Always-visible "Skip" button (keyboard: Esc/Space). Skipping jumps to the outcome screen.
- Calm mode or `prefers-reduced-motion`: use the `calmFile` versions and disable all UI shake.
- Duck the ambient music to ~15% during cutscenes; restore afterwards.

### 6.2 Outcome → cutscene mapping (implement exactly; the engine decides the failure point)

| Outcome | Sequence | Overlay at the end |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough thrust (TWR < 1.2) | `launch` with `stopAt: 1.2` → freeze, slow zoom-out | "We didn't leave the pad!" + `fail_womp.wav` |
| Structural / pressure failure, over max launch mass | `launch` → `explosion` | "Rapid unscheduled disassembly" + `fail_womp.wav` |
| Out of fuel / Δv short in transit, or Deep-Space Drive range too short | `launch` → `cruising` (`stopAt: 3.5`) → freeze with engine-sputter flicker | "Out of fuel" + `fail_womp.wav` |
| Comms range too short | `launch` → `cruising` → freeze into a TV-static/glitch effect (CSS/canvas) | "Signal lost" + `fail_womp.wav` |

| Full success | `launch` → `cruising` → `planet` → short "Mission accomplished at <nickname>!" stamp → `cruising` (`mirror: true`) → splashdown celebration screen | `success_fanfare.wav` + confetti |

### 7.2 Derived values (label "calculated from NASA data")

- Planet type rule (gameplay simplification, say so): radius ≤ 1.6 Earth radii → "probably rocky" → **land and return**; larger → "probably gas/ice-rich, no solid surface" → **orbit survey and return**. Allow per-planet overrides in 7.3.

- The archive has no atmosphere data for most planets: show "Atmosphere: not measured" unless a fact below provides it.

## 8. Rocket workshop (drag and drop)

### 8.1 Components (`src/data/components.ts`)
Each has `id, name, category, cost, massKg, description, funDescription` (kid-friendly one-liner) plus stats. Give 3–4 options per category with real trade-offs (cheap/heavy/weak ↔ expensive/light/strong). Names can be playful (e.g. "Sparky Solid Booster", "HydroLox Hero"), but stats must be realistic for real technology. Fictional parts carry `fictional: true` and a visible **"Imaginary tech"** badge.

Slots:
1. **Payload pod** (required): capacity, crew seats 0–4, life support. Astronauts (0–4, chosen with a stepper and little astronaut avatars) need seats.
2. **Launch engine** (required, Thrusters): thrust (kN), specific impulse Isp (s). Realistic: solid ≈ 250 s, kerosene/LOX ≈ 280–310 s, hydrogen/LOX ≈ 420–450 s.
3. **Deep-Space Drive** (required, Thrusters, `fictional: true`): range in light-years. This is the honest stand-in for interstellar travel, which no real spacecraft can do today. Explain this in a tooltip: even the nearest exoplanet would take Voyager 1 tens of thousands of years.
4. **Fuel tanks** (required, stack 1–3): propellant mass, dry mass, propellant type (must match the engine).
5. **Comms dish** (required): range in light-years.
6. **Pressure system**: pressurisation type and capacity; must suit the tanks and engine.
7. **Heat & radiation shield**: max temperature (K), radiation rating.

## 10. Mission engine, difficulty and scoring

### 10.1 Engine (`src/engine/`, pure TypeScript, no React, fully unit-tested)
`evaluateMission(design, planet, difficulty, crew)` returns per-phase pass/fail, the first failure point and cause, every number used, and the score breakdown. Show the formulas in a "How the maths works" panel.

- **Δv (Tsiolkovsky)**: Δv = Isp × 9.81 × ln(m₀ / m_f); m₀ = fully fuelled mass, m_f = dry mass.
- **Lift-off**: TWR = thrust / (m₀ × 9.81). Fail if < 1.2. Also fail if over max launch mass, or pressure system incompatible/under capacity.
- **Δv needed (chemical phases only)**: Earth launch to orbit ≈ 9.4 km/s (real approximate figure, including losses); rocky planet landing + ascent ≈ 2 × 1.2 × planet orbital speed; orbit survey = insertion + departure at a stated fraction of orbital speed; Earth return = small fixed correction budget (re-entry handled by the shield). Add a 10% margin. Document every assumption.
- **Transit**: Deep-Space Drive range ≥ 2 × distance (gameplay assumption, labelled).
- **Comms**: range ≥ distance. **Heat**: shield ≥ planet equilibrium temperature, plus an Earth re-entry requirement. **Crew**: each astronaut adds fixed mass; crewed flights need radiation rating ≥ threshold.

### 10.2 Difficulty (adds decisions, not just less money)
- **Cadet**: big budget; checks: lift-off, Δv, drive range, budget. Comms, pressure and shields auto-pass with a tooltip explaining they're simplified here. Cass gives strong hints and a "suggested build" button that fills 3 of the slots.
- **Engineer**: medium budget; adds comms and heat checks; orbit-survey missions; hints on request only.
- **Flight Director**: small budget; all checks including pressure compatibility and crew radiation; no suggested builds; over budget blocks launch.

### 10.3 Score (per launch)
Base 0–1000: mission success 400 (partial credit by phase reached: lift-off 50, transit 150, arrival 250, return 350); performance margin 200 (rewards Δv and TWR close to what's needed; penalise both under- and heavy over-building); budget efficiency 200; component suitability 100; resource balance 100.
**Astronaut multiplier**: success × (1 + 0.1 × crew); failure × (1 − 0.15 × crew), minimum 0. Maximum per launch = 1400. Show the multiplier before launch so it's a deliberate gamble.
Run score = average of the 10 launches, one decimal place.

### 10.4 "Why it happened" feedback
List every check with ✓/✗, the actual numbers, and one plain-English sentence on what to change, e.g. "Your rocket had 11.2 km/s of power but this trip needed 13.8 km/s. Add a fuel tank or pick a more efficient engine (higher Isp)." Add a "Real science" footnote linking the relevant NASA concept where useful. Tone: encouraging, never mocking.
